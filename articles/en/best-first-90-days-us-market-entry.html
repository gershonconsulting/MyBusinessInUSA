The Best First 90 Days of a US Market Entry: A Practical Plan

2026-10-10 · Gershon Consulting

The first 90 days of a US market entry set the tone for everything that follows. Founders who spend that time polishing a website or waiting for a trade show often reach month four with no real conversations. Founders who treat the first quarter as a structured experiment usually come out with a sharper target, a handful of qualified opportunities and real evidence about what American buyers will pay for. Here is the best way to organize those 90 days.

Why a 90-day plan matters

The US is not one market but many. The SBA Office of Advocacy counts more than 36.2 million small businesses in the country, representing 99.9% of all firms, and that is before counting mid-market and enterprise accounts. Without focus, an international team can spend months talking to companies that will never buy. At the same time, buyers are harder to reach: a 2025 Gartner survey found that 61% of B2B buyers prefer a buying experience without a sales rep, and 73% avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach. A plan forces focus and relevance from day one.

Days 1 to 30: define the US buyer

The first month is about the ideal customer profile (ICP). It is the single most important decision of the launch, and it is rarely a copy of the profile that works at home.

  • Pick one or two segments. Choose by industry, company size and region. A narrow start is the best way to learn fast.
  • Name the decision-maker. Identify the titles that own the budget and the titles that influence the choice.
  • Translate your value into US terms. Express the benefit in dollars, hours or risk reduced, with US-relevant examples.
  • Build a first list. A few hundred well-chosen accounts beat thousands of loosely matched ones.

Days 31 to 60: book the first meetings

Month two is about starting real conversations. LinkedIn is a natural channel here: the company reports more than 290 million members across the United States and Canada. Combined with email, it gives an international founder a direct line to US decision-makers without a local office.

  1. Warm up the profile. Make sure the founder's and the company's LinkedIn pages speak to US buyers, in American English.
  2. Run short sequences. A connection request, a brief message, a follow-up and an email work better than long pitches.
  3. Personalize at the segment level. Each segment gets its own angle and proof point.
  4. Qualify before the call. Confirm role, need and timing so that meetings are worth the founder's time.

Days 61 to 90: turn meetings into pipeline

By the third month, the goal shifts from activity to evidence. Track every meeting in a simple CRM and look at the pattern behind them.

QuestionWhat it tells you
Which segment replied most?Where to double down next quarter
Which objections came up repeatedly?What to fix in the offer or messaging
Which meetings led to a second call?Which titles really drive the decision
What price reactions did you hear?Whether your US pricing holds

At day 90, the best outcome is not a signed contract (though that happens). It is a validated ICP, a repeatable outreach approach and a pipeline you can forecast.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Targeting "all of America" instead of one region or vertical.
  • Translating home-market sales material word for word.
  • Stopping outreach after two weeks because replies are slow.
  • Letting meetings go without a clear next step and owner.
